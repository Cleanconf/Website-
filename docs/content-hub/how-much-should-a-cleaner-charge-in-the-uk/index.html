How Much Should a Cleaner Charge in the UK

Published 12 November 2026 · 2 min read · by the OSO Organised team

Pricing by guesswork usually means underpricing. Here's a method that starts from what you need to earn, not what feels easiest to say out loud.

Start with your take-home target

Decide what you want to take home each month. Add your costs: products, travel, insurance, and tax put aside. Divide the total by the hours you can realistically bill, not the hours you work including travel and admin.

As a sense check, the National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour from April 2026, according to GOV.UK. Your rate as a self-employed cleaner should cover a lot more than that, since you're also covering your own costs, tax and the time admin takes.

Know your VAT threshold

Most solo cleaners are well under this, but it's worth knowing: you need to register for VAT once your turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, per GOV.UK. Keep an eye on it as your client list grows.

Factor in your travel

If you drive between jobs, the simplified mileage rate from GOV.UK is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles a year, for cars and vans, from 6 April 2026. Whether or not you claim it this way, it's a useful number for working out what a job actually costs you to get to.

Price each type of clean separately

A regular clean and a deep clean take different time and effort, so price them differently. One illustrative starting point: set your base hourly rate for a regular clean, and price a deep clean at that rate plus 25 to 50 percent to reflect the extra time. Treat these as a starting point to adjust for your own area and experience, not a rule.
