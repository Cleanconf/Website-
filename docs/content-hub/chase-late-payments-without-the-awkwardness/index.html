How to chase late payments without the awkwardness

Published 8 September 2026 · 4 min read · by the OSO Organised team

Nobody starts a cleaning business because they love asking people for money. But late payments add up fast, and the stress of an unpaid job can follow you around all week.

The good news: getting paid on time is mostly about a few small habits, not difficult conversations. Here's how to do it kindly.

Why it feels awkward

You're in someone's home. You might like them. It feels personal, and bringing up money can feel like you're accusing them of something. Most of the time, though, a late payment is just a forgotten one. A gentle reminder is a favour, not a confrontation.

Agree terms up front

The best way to avoid an awkward chase is to be clear before you start. When you take on a new client, tell them plainly:

  • What you charge
  • When payment is due (for example, on the day of the clean, or within 3 days)
  • How to pay (bank transfer, cash, and your details)

Say it once, warmly, and you've set the tone for everything that follows.

Send a friendly first reminder

If a payment hasn't arrived a day or two after it was due, send something light. Assume they simply forgot.

Hi [name], hope you're well! Just a quick reminder that this week's clean (£[amount]) is still outstanding. Bank details are below if that's easiest. Thanks so much! — [your name]

Follow up with a clear message

If there's still nothing after another few days, it's fine to be a little firmer while staying polite.

Hi [name], following up on my message from [day]. The payment of £[amount] for [date]'s clean is now [number] days overdue. Could you let me know when I can expect it? Happy to resend my details. Thanks, [your name]

Make paying easy

The fewer steps between "I should pay" and "done", the faster you get paid. Put your bank details at the bottom of every reminder. If you send a written summary of the job, include the amount and the due date on it.

Know when to pause

If a client regularly pays very late and doesn't respond to reminders, it's reasonable to let them know you'll hold the next visit until the account is settled. Keep it matter-of-fact:

Hi [name], I've not been able to reach you about the outstanding £[amount]. I'll need to pause our next clean on [date] until that's sorted. Let me know and we'll get straight back to normal.

  • Agree terms before the first clean
  • Send a light reminder a day or two after the due date
  • Follow up clearly if needed
  • Always make paying a one-tap job
