How to organise a cleaning business

Published 8 September 2026 · 5 min read · by the OSO Organised team

When you clean for a living, the cleaning is the easy part. The hard part is everything around it: remembering who's booked in on Thursday, knowing whether Mrs Patel paid last week, and working out if you can actually afford to take Friday off.

Most people run all of that from memory, a notebook and their phone. It works — until a busy week, a poorly child, or a client who swears they already paid. Here's a simple system that holds up when things get hectic.

1. Keep one client list — and only one

Pick a single place for every client and stick to it. For each one, write down:

  • Name and address
  • Phone number and how they like to be contacted
  • How to get in (key safe code, where the key lives, who's home)
  • What they've asked for (kitchen done last, don't move the ornaments, dog is friendly)
  • What you charge and how often you visit

The goal is simple: never arrive at a job trying to remember a code or a preference. It's all in one place, and you can hand it over if someone covers for you.

2. Put every booking in a calendar

Your calendar should show your whole week at a glance, including the fortnightly and monthly jobs that are easiest to forget. Add recurring visits once so you're not rewriting the same names every week.

Two rules keep a calendar trustworthy: put a booking in the moment it's agreed, and check tomorrow's jobs the night before. That's it.

3. Track payments as you go, not at month end

The moment a payment lands, mark that job as paid. Don't leave it for later — later is when it gets lost. Keep a running list of what's still outstanding so you always know the number without doing sums.

If a payment is late, a short friendly message usually sorts it. We've written some you can copy in our guide on chasing late payments without the awkwardness.

4. Build a five-minute weekly admin habit

Once a week — same time, same cuppa — do a quick sweep:

  • Check next week's calendar is right
  • Mark any payments that came in
  • Send reminders for anything overdue
  • Add any new clients or changes

Five minutes a week beats two hours on a Sunday night every time.

5. Let one app do the heavy lifting

A notebook and three apps can work, but they don't talk to each other, so you end up copying things across. That's where mistakes creep in.
